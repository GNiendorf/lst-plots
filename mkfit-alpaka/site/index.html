Where it is in #290

Two commits: “Seeds and the LST hand-off” (MkFitAlpakaLstInputProducer, PixSeedKernels.h) and “Hit input and EventOfHits” (MkFitAlpakaOTRecHitsProducer). The asynchronous LST change is a different thing.

What it waits for

The refit uses mkFit's propagation and Kalman update on the GPU, and the same fit then gives mkFit its starting seeds. In the proposed plan this is PR L3. It needs only M1, MkFitCore's portable physics, not the rest of the mkFit work, so it can merge right after M1, around December, ahead of the GPU building and fit.

Could it come even sooner?

Only without mkFit's propagation. Two ways, neither proposed:

  • Use Patatrack's own track parameters for LST's pixel seeds, with no refit. No mkFit code and a small PR, but LST's pixel seeds would carry different momentum and charge estimates from the ones LST is tuned on. It needs a physics check of LST, and possibly retuning.
  • A small GPU refit written just for the seeds. No mkFit code either, but a third copy of the propagation and update math, which is the maintainability problem the one-mkFit study is solving.

The proposed plan avoids both: L3 uses the portable propagation that M1 puts into MkFitCore, which is already in CMSSW.

Two terms used below

chi2 of a hit: how far the hit is from where the track predicts it, measured in units of the expected error (squared). A good hit gives about 1; both the KF fit and this port call a hit an outlier above 20.

Inside and outside fits: the final fit runs once from the inner end outwards and once from the outer end inwards. Combining them gives, at every hit, a prediction from the hits on one side and from the hits on the other side.

DEV-3better fitends up as a MkFitCore fit option

The first and last hit can be outliers too, and removal is repeated

mkFit can never remove the first or last hit of a track, and it checks for outliers only once. The port tests the end hits as the KF fit does, and repeats removal and refit.

mkFit today

A hit is an outlier only when both the inside and the outside fit disagree with it (for pT > 1 GeV: both chi2 above 8 and their sum above 20). At an end hit one of the two fits has just started from a deliberately vague state, so it always agrees and the hit is never removed. There is one round, and the refit is never checked again.

This PR

An end hit is an outlier when the prediction from all the other hits gives a chi2 above 20, the KF fit's cut. After removing hits the track is refitted and tested again.

Why

A wrong hit at the end of a track pulls the whole fit, and the KF fit removes such hits. Leaving them in is the largest single reason the mkFit fit loses efficiency against the KF menu.

Effect

Together with DEV-4 and DEV-5: efficiency +0.27% (ttbar) and +0.26% (QCD) compared with the mkFit fit, with a lower fake rate. No validation plot bin gets worse by more than 2σ (500 ttbar + 500 QCD events).

DEV-10better fit · the largest single gainends up as a MkFitCore fit option

Hits near the outer end can be outliers, one per round until none are left

Even with DEV-3, hits 2 and 3 from the outer end can never be removed, and all flagged hits are removed at once. The port tests the three outermost hits and removes one hit per round, largest first, until a round removes nothing, as the KF fit does.

mkFit today

Near the outer end the outside fit is built from only one or two hits plus the vague starting state, so it can never disagree with a hit there. All hits flagged in a round go at once, so one bad hit can get a good neighbour removed with it.

This PR

The three hits nearest the outer end are tested with the prediction from the inside (chi2 above 20), but only when the hits before them lie on at least three other detector layers, since three points are needed to fix the track's circle. One hit is removed per round, the worst first. Rounds continue until one removes nothing, and at least three hits always stay.

Why

The KF fit (EstimateCut 20) removes one outlier per iteration, near-end hits included, and has no iteration cap. One extra rule protects short displaced tracks: a hit flagged only by this new test counts only in a round where no other hit of the track is flagged. Without it, good outer hits were removed at 31.6–63 cm production radius (−0.8% ttbar, −1.0% QCD there).

Effect

Efficiency +0.16 ± 0.01% (ttbar) and +0.18 ± 0.02% (QCD), on 2,000 + 1,000 events; fake rate 0.98 → 0.92% and 1.22 → 1.17%; pull widths unchanged. The gap to the KF menu goes from −0.22 to −0.06% (ttbar) and from −0.30 to −0.12% (QCD).

More detail: rounds, GPU kernels and the inner end
  • Letting rounds continue until nothing is removed (instead of stopping at 6) adds +0.044 ± 0.014% (ttbar) and +0.039 ± 0.010% (QCD) efficiency. Only 0.18% and 0.19% of tracks need more than 6 rounds; the most any track needed was 16 (ttbar) and 18 (QCD).
  • On the GPU, the first 6 rounds run as separate kernels; the few tracks still refitting then finish in one extra kernel with the same steps per track.
  • At the inner end only the end hit is tested (DEV-3). Testing three hits there made the distance-to-beam-line resolution 1–3% worse, and 1.1% of tracks lost a pixel hit.

DEV-5better fitends up as a MkFitCore fix

A refit moves the track to its first hit before using it

Each fit pass assumes it already sits on its first hit and skips the step that moves the track there. After the first hit has been removed as an outlier, that assumption is wrong, and the first update is made in the wrong place. The port moves the track to the first hit first.

mkFit today

The first hit of each pass is used without propagating to it, as if the track were already on that hit's surface. After the innermost hit is removed, the refit starts from a state that is not on the new first hit: 5.0% of refits are off by a median of 0.68 cm, the first step's chi2 has a median of about 2,200 instead of about 1, and 4.4% of those fits end with a broken error matrix.

This PR

Refits after outlier removal propagate to their first hit before the update, like every other hit.

Why

It fixes a wrong assumption that only shows up once outliers are removed, so it matters more with DEV-3 and DEV-10. It also makes the result less sensitive to rounding, which helps CPU and GPU agree.

Effect

First-step chi2 median 2,200 → 0.93; fits with a negative or NaN chi2 71 → 6 per 200 ttbar events (in MkFitCore with the same change). Efficiency +0.024% in MkFitCore, +0.005% in the port. CPU-vs-GPU hit-list differences 98 → 16 per 193,000 tracks.

DEV-4better fitends up as a MkFitCore / converter option

Tracks whose fit quality is impossible are dropped

A chi2 can only be negative (or NaN) when the track's error matrix is broken. mkFit still turns such a candidate into a track; the KF fit rejects it. The port drops it.

mkFit today

The output converter converts any candidate that passes its state checks, whatever its chi2.

This PR

The converter skips candidates with a negative or non-finite chi2, after its existing check that the error matrix is positive-definite.

Why

Such a track has meaningless errors, which downstream users (vertexing, PF) trust. The KF fit does not produce them.

Effect

2 tracks in 500 ttbar events; no measurable efficiency change.

Full technical note

  Evidence: 2 tracks in 500 ttbar events; its share of the DEV-3 gain is ~0.
DEV-1bug fixends up as a small MkFitCore PR

NaN checks that the compiler removed

MkFitCore's final fit has two checks meant to catch tracks whose fit produced NaN. MkFitCore is compiled with -Ofast, which tells the compiler NaN never occurs, so the compiler deletes both checks. The port keeps them working.

mkFit today

MkFitter.cc:102 skips a track whose chi2 is NaN, and MkBuilder.cc:1545 replaces a NaN chi2 with 106. Under -Ofast both are folded away: NaN track parameters are written out, and NaN hit chi2 enter the outlier ranking.

This PR

The same checks, in code compiled without the "no NaN" assumption, so they run.

Why

A track with NaN parameters is garbage for every downstream user, and a NaN in the ranking makes the outlier choice arbitrary.

Effect

100 ttbar events, 193,069 fitted tracks: 59 with NaN parameters in MkFitCore, 0 in the port. The fix for MkFitCore is a NaN test that survives -Ofast; after it, both agree.

LST's candidates get their starting state on the GPU

Before building, each LST candidate (T5, T4, pT3, pT5) needs a starting track state: position, momentum and their errors. Today the CPU computes it with CMSSW's seed creator. The port does the same steps on the GPU, but with mkFit's propagation and material instead of CMSSW's.

mkFit today

LSTOutputConverter runs CMSSW's seed creator on each candidate: a helix through three of its points, starting errors, then one Kalman pass over its hits with CMSSW's propagator. It is the largest CPU cost of the step.

This PR

The same structure on the GPU: the same helix, the same starting errors, one pass over the hits, the state at the last hit and the same rejections. Only the propagation and material come from mkFit. As in CMSSW, a rejected T5 or T4 is dropped and a rejected pT3 or pT5 keeps its pixel seed, hits and state.

Why

CMSSW's propagator is not available on the GPU; mkFit's is, and the building already uses it.

Effect

Efficiency −0.013% (ttbar) and −0.014% (QCD), ± 0.001%. No validation plot bin moves by more than 2σ; fake and duplicate rates equal or lower.

DEV-8moved to the GPUpart of the GPU seed hand-off

Hits with equal sort keys keep their input order

When LST candidates become mkFit seeds, their hits are sorted: inner tracker first, then by subdetector and radius. Hits on overlapping modules of the same layer have equal keys, and for lists longer than 16 hits their final order depends on the internals of the C++ sort. The port keeps such hits in input order, which the GPU can reproduce exactly.

mkFit today

LSTOutputConverter uses std::sort. It keeps the input order for equal keys in lists up to 16 hits; for longer lists the order of ties follows the sort algorithm's internals.

This PR

Equal keys always keep their input order, the same as a stable sort. The GPU gets there without sorting: each hit's rank within its LST layer.

Why

An order that depends on a library's internals cannot be reproduced on the GPU. Input order can, and it makes the result identical on CPU and GPU.

Effect

Seeds identical to a CPU stable sort (728,000 seeds). Against today's order, 1.4% (ttbar) and 1.5% (QCD) of seeds have a different hit order, all with more than 16 hits. Efficiency +0.01 ± 0.01% and +0.02 ± 0.02%: no change.

How it runs

Following thousands of candidates at once

Track building follows every track candidate through the detector, layer by layer: predict where it crosses the next layer, look for hits there, keep the best continuations. A ttbar event at 200 pileup has thousands of candidates. MkFitCore shares that work among CPU cores and their vector units; the port shares it among thousands of GPU threads. The arithmetic for one candidate is the same; the way the work is organised is not.

MkFitCore, on the CPU
  1. Split the event. Seeds are grouped by η region and handed to the CPU threads in tasks of 32 seeds (TBB).
  2. 8 candidates per instruction. Inside a task, candidates go 8 at a time. Their parameters sit side by side in memory (a "Matriplex"), so one AVX2 vector instruction does the same step for all 8.
  3. For each layer: propagate 8 at once → find compatible hits, one candidate at a time (the code notes that vectorising this makes it slower) → chi2 of those hits, 8 at once → for each seed, sort the options and keep the best few → update the survivors, 8 at once.
  4. Data: growing containers (std::vector), sorts, maps and callbacks.
The port, on the GPU
  1. The whole event at once. No regions or tasks: every seed and candidate of the event sits in GPU memory.
  2. One thread per item. Each step is a kernel with one GPU thread per seed, per candidate, or per candidate–hit pair.
  3. For each layer, a fixed chain of six kernels: set up the candidates (thread per seed) → find compatible hits (16 threads per candidate) → chi2 (thread per candidate–hit pair) → accept or reject each option (thread per candidate) → keep the best few (thread per seed, a short running top list instead of a sort) → update (thread per survivor).
  4. Data: fixed-size arrays sized once per event, no sorting, and the CPU never waits between kernels.

On the CPU (Alpaka's serial backend) the port runs the same kernels as plain loops on one thread; propagation, the hit-search window, the Kalman update and the final fit go 8 candidates per instruction, as in MkFitCore. A ttbar event goes through about 46 layer steps, so building is about 390 kernel launches per event.

In short

Three reasons

1

No objects made only to be converted

The mkFit-fit menu builds CMSSW objects (legacy rechits, TrajectorySeeds) and then converts them into mkFit's own format. The port writes mkFit's format directly and makes legacy objects only where something downstream reads them.

2

Seed fits in mkFit's matrix code

Both menus fit every seed with a Kalman filter. The mkFit-fit menu uses CMSSW's general-purpose seed creator, one seed and one hit object at a time through virtual interfaces. The port uses mkFit's matrix code, 8 seeds per vector instruction.

3

Flat tables instead of class hierarchies

Geometry, local-position (CPE) and navigation lookups go through small flat tables built once per run, instead of the virtual detector-layer and CPE classes. An earlier profile measured 17 MB of fresh memory touched per event by the port's tracking libraries vs 57 MB in the mkFit-fit menu's chain.

mkFit-fit menu

What it means

Most of this doesn't need a GPU

The savings around mkFit come from the data flow: fewer conversions, vectorised seed fits, flat tables. They are why the port's CPU path beats the existing CPU menu even though its track building is not faster. They also make the GPU path possible, because every one of these steps now produces data a GPU can read.

How it stays correct

Proving "no output change"

M1 and M2 must leave every existing mkFit output bit for bit unchanged. Source-level arguments are not enough, because MkFitCore is built with link-time optimisation: the compiler decides at link time what to inline, and inlining changes floating-point rounding.

  • Machine code firstCompare the linked MkFitCore library before and after, function by function. Identical machine code means identical output for every input.
  • Then outputsFor any function whose machine code changes: bitwise comparison of Run-3 offline, Run-3 HLT, Phase-2 offline and Phase-2 HLT outputs, plus mkFit's own standalone validation.
  • Constants stay constantsValues that are compile-time constants in MkFitCore today stay that way (they work on the GPU too); only true runtime settings are passed in.
  • CPU and GPU builds kept apartEach backend's copy of a shared template gets its own symbol, so a CPU library built with -Ofast and a GPU library's host code can never swap functions at load time.

Known limit: in a few places the port writes explicit fused multiply-adds so that its CPU build matches MkFitCore exactly. Where code is shared, the CPU result stays MkFitCore's and the GPU agrees with it to rounding, as today.

Alternatives

Why not replace MkFitCore with the port in one go?

That is M4 done first: the port's driver runs every mkFit configuration, on CPU and GPU, and MkFitCore's CPU driver is deleted. It gives the same end state with a single driver. The work is quick; what makes it harder is that it touches everyone else at once.

What the port would need first

  • Hit selection V1 (10 Run-3 iterations)
  • The strip charge cut and propagation inside a layer (all 12 Run-3 iterations)
  • Hit masks (Run-3 offline iterations after the first)
  • Run-3 seed partitioning and seed cleaning; 4 quality filters; one duplicate cleaner; seed-hit sorting
  • Room for 6 candidates per seed (Phase-2 offline)
  • Run-3 hit inputs and the output converter that offline uses

With today's module defaults, 15 of the 16 mkFit configurations in the release use at least one of these; only our LST step uses none.

What it would change for others

  • Every mkFit user's tracks shift slightly (rounding and tie order), so Run 3, Phase-2 offline and the HLT need tracking sign-off at once.
  • mkFit's standalone tools (12.6k lines) must be moved onto the new driver.
  • The mkFit developers' CPU driver, which they tuned for years, is retired.

The step-by-step plan reaches one copy of the physics and decisions without any of this, and leaves M4 as a later, separate decision.
